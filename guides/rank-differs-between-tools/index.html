Guides for checking Amazon keyword rank

Why is my Amazon keyword rank different in different tools?

Updated November 9, 2026 · Milton Styles LLC

Short answer

Two tools can both be right and still disagree. They usually differ on one or more of these: when the search was made (or whether the number is an average), where it was made from, signed in or out, what counts as a position (ads included or not), how deep the tool looks, and how it handles variation listings. Device matters too: the phone app and a computer lay out results differently.

Amazon's results also move on their own, even minutes apart. Pick one tool and one setup, and read the trend rather than any single number.

1. When was the number taken?

This is the most common reason. Tools fall into three groups:

  • Live checks search Amazon at the moment you ask, so the number is the current position.
  • Stored snapshots search once a day, or a few times a day, and show you the latest saved result, which can be hours old.
  • Averages show a figure built from several days of data. Averages smooth out the noise, but they can lag a real change by days and can keep showing a rank for a while after a product went out of stock.

If one tool says 8 and another says 15, first check whether you are comparing this morning with yesterday, or today with a weekly average.

2. Where was the search made from?

Amazon tailors results to the Deliver-to location (zip or postcode) and to the location the search comes from. Delivering to New York or to Los Angeles, or searching the US site from another country, can show different organic and sponsored results for the same keyword at the same moment. Many tools search from data centres or a fixed city and do not tell you which.

3. Signed in or signed out?

A signed-in search follows the account's shopping history; a signed-out search is not tied to one account's history. Sign-in also changes which ads appear and how many, which moves every position below them. Your phone app is almost always signed in, which is one reason it rarely matches a tool.

4. What counts as a position?

Some tools count every slot on the page, ads included. Others count organic results only. A brand banner or a carousel may count as one slot, or not at all. Because the number of ads above your listing changes from search to search, the count with ads included jumps around much more.

The ads-included count moves more, because the number of ads above a listing changes from one search to the next; the organic-only count stays put when only the ads change. An ads-included position and an organic-only position answer two different questions, so they should not be compared with each other.

5. How deep does the tool look?

Tools check a fixed number of pages, often one to three, sometimes more. Below that depth they show the keyword as unranked, or keep an older value. A product at position 70 is "not ranked" in a tool that reads one page and "70" in one that reads three. Pages also hold different numbers of results depending on the layout Amazon serves.

6. Variation listings come and go

If your product has variations (sizes, colours, pack counts), Amazon often shows one child of the family for a search and a different one a few hours later. A tool tracking one child will see it vanish and come back, and two tools checking at different times will disagree about whether it ranks at all.

We measured this on one brand's catalog: two months of checks, three times a day, on amazon.com (16 child ASINs in 6 variation families, about 5,500 keywords, signed out, ZIP 90001, August 26 to October 26, 2026).

  • A child ranking in the top 30 was found again at the next check, 4 hours later, about 97% of the time.
  • The family as a whole (any of its children) was found again more than 99% of the time.
  • When a child did go missing, a sibling from the same family was ranking in the top 30 for that keyword about three times out of four. In most of those cases that sibling was already ranking; an already-ranking sibling moving up into the vacated position was uncommon, about 2% of those missing checks.

"Missing" here means not found anywhere in the first three pages (about 144 organic results). Rare per keyword is still routine across a list: at about 2.7 in 100 per recheck, a seller tracking 1,000 top-30 keyword and ASIN pairs will see about 27 seem to disappear between two checks.

Track every child in the family and look at the family's best position before reading a disappearance as a ranking loss.

Amazon moves on its own, too

Even with the same tool, location, sign-in and counting rule, results shift between checks minutes apart. Small differences (a few places) are normal. Treat a one-off move as noise until it repeats.

How do I get rank numbers I can compare?

  1. Use one tool for the trend you report.
  2. Keep the same marketplace, Deliver-to postcode and sign-in state every time.
  3. Know whether the number includes ads, and use an organic-only count for organic progress.
  4. Check at similar times of day, or several times a day and average them.
  5. Track every child of a variation family.
  6. When you compare two tools, compare them on the same keyword at the same time and find out how each one counts.

Which number is right?

Often both. Each is a correct answer to a slightly different question: a different moment, location, sign-in state or counting rule. The useful question is which setup matches the decision you are making, and then keeping that setup fixed.

Quick questions

Why does my phone show a different rank from my tool?

The app is usually signed in, follows your shopping history, and lays out a different number of results per screen.

Why does my rank differ from my VA's or my team's?

Different Deliver-to addresses, different countries, and different sign-in states. Agree on one setup.

Is an average rank wrong?

No, it answers a different question. It is steadier, but slower to show a real change.
