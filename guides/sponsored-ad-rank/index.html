Guides for checking Amazon keyword rank

How do I track where my sponsored ads rank in Amazon search?

Updated November 10, 2026 · Milton Styles LLC

Short answer

Amazon's ad console does not tell you which slot your ad took on a particular search. It reports placement groups (top of search, rest of search, product pages), the share of eligible top-of-search impressions your campaign received, and, for a search term, your impression share and impression rank within that ad type. Those numbers compare you with other advertisers in aggregate; they do not name competitors or show their ads.

To see where ads actually appear, yours and your competitors', look at the search results page itself, by hand or with a tool that records each placement and its type, and check the same way every time. Amazon changes the ads it shows with the delivery location, where the search is made from, whether you are signed in, and the time of day; how often to check is covered below.

Why does it matter where sponsored ads show?

For the search terms you care about, the console tells you what you spent and what you got, but not what a shopper saw. Seeing the page, for your own listings and for competitors', helps in three ways:

  • Tuning bids. Where your ad lands, next to where your organic listing sits, shows whether a bid or placement adjustment changed what shoppers see.
  • Reading competitors. Which brands advertise on a term, with which ad types, how high, and at what time of day, is public. It shows where they chose to advertise (it does not show their bids).
  • Research before a launch. Before you sell in a niche, the same view shows how crowded its key terms are with ads and which ad types the leading brands use.

Some goals sellers pursue with this view (examples, not recommendations; the right choice depends on margins and goals):

  • Already ranking near the top organically? Some sellers keep their top-of-search adjustment low and check whether a sponsored tile still shows farther down the page, for a second spot without paying for the top one.
  • Ranking low organically? Some raise the bid or the top-of-search adjustment to compete for the top of the page, to be seen at all on that term.
  • Testing ad types: a Sponsored Products video may draw a different response from a Sponsored Brands video on the same term. Knowing which one appeared, where, for you and for competitors, is what makes that comparison possible.

Amazon does not sell a particular row, such as "just below my organic listing". Bids and adjustments change your chances in a placement group; the live page shows where the ad actually landed.

What does Amazon's ad console tell you about ad position?

Three reports come close to "where does my ad show", and each answers a different question:

For Sponsored Products the console names the groups "Top of search (first page)", "Rest of search" and "Product pages"; it does not list which rows fall in each group. Product-page placements are not in the search results at all. Amazon Business placements are reported separately. Amazon's launch notes define top-of-search impression share and the search term impression share report. All three are templates on the unified Reporting page of the ad console, which replaces the older sponsored ads reports page at the end of 2026.

These are summaries over many searches, of your own ads. None of them can tell you that your ad was the fourth result on page 1 for "garlic press", delivering to Los Angeles, checked at 9 a.m., or that a competitor's video sat just above it. That is the question most sellers are asking when they say "ad rank".

Not every sponsored spot on a search page is something you buy directly. Telling them apart shows which ones your bids move.

Direct placements are where a bid change can be tested and its effect measured. Themed rows are still worth tracking: they show whether Amazon is choosing to show your product, or a competitor's, in that extra spot.

Two kinds of video ad

A video on the search page can be either of two different ads, bought in different campaign types:

  • Sponsored Brands video is the long-standing brand video format, bought in a Sponsored Brands campaign.
  • Sponsored Products video is a newer Sponsored Products format. You add product feature videos to a Sponsored Products ad group, or opt in and let Amazon assemble one from your existing product videos, so the regular product tile plays a video. Same targeting, budget and cost-per-click bid, plus the optional video bid adjustment. Amazon's help article "Understand Sponsored Products video" (October 20, 2026) lists it as available in the US only, and not for books; the Sponsored Products video guide covers setup.

Many reports and tools record both simply as "video". Because they are bought and bid differently, it pays to note which one appeared, for your ads and for competitors'. In our own checks, Sponsored Products video showed up more often in signed-in searches.

Why is a sponsored position a moving target?

Amazon picks the ads for each search as it happens, so there is no single fixed ad rank for a keyword. What a shopper sees changes with:

  • Delivery location. The Deliver-to address (zip or postcode) changes both the organic results and the ads.
  • Where the search is made from. Searching the same marketplace from another state or country can show different results. This is why your numbers and your VA's can differ, even with the same Deliver-to address.
  • Signed in or signed out. Signed-in searches follow that shopping account's history, and in our checks some ad types (Sponsored Products video among them) showed up more when signed in.
  • Time of day. Budgets run out, ad schedules switch campaigns on and off, and advertisers' bids change during the day.
  • Device. The app and the mobile site lay out results differently from a computer.

So a single check is a snapshot. A useful sponsored rank is a series of checks made the same way, to tell signal from noise: repeated checks show how much a position normally moves on that term, so you can recognise a real change when it comes. Averaging checks taken close together, such as several in one day, steadies the number. Averaging over many days goes too far the other way: it hides real changes for days and can keep showing a rank long after a product has gone out of stock.

How do I check sponsored positions by hand?

  1. Decide whether to search signed in or signed out. Use a private window (signed out) for a view that is not tied to one shopping account's history, or stay signed in when you need to see the ads tied to that shopping account. Keep the same choice every time.
  2. Set Deliver-to to a postcode in the region you care about, and keep it the same between checks. Search from the same location each time, too.
  3. Search the keyword on the marketplace site you sell or research in.
  4. Count the slots from the top, ads included: the brand banner at the top, sponsored product tiles mixed into the grid, video placements (note whether each is a Sponsored Brands video or a Sponsored Products video), store rows and themed carousels further down. Note the page number too.
  5. Write down the keyword, time, page, position, placement type and brand, for your own listings and for the competitors you follow, then repeat at other times of day.

Don't click your own ads, or competitors' ads, while checking. On a cost-per-click ad a click is spend, and clicks can affect what that signed-in account sees next.

Checking by hand works for a handful of keywords. It gets slow beyond that, and your own searching history starts to colour the results when you are signed in.

How should I count positions?

Pick one counting rule and stick to it. A common rule is to count every slot in order, ads included, and record the placement type next to the position. A brand banner or a carousel counts as one slot, however many products it holds. For organic results it also helps to keep a second count that skips the ads, because the number of ads above a listing changes from search to search.

How do I track sponsored rank for many keywords?

Use a tool that loads the search pages and records every placement it finds, for any ASIN you list, yours or a competitor's. Whatever you choose, check that it:

  • records each placement type separately (sponsored product, Sponsored Products video, brand collection, Sponsored Brands video, store, themed carousel), not only "sponsored";
  • lets you set and keep consistent the Deliver-to location, so checks are comparable;
  • searches signed in or signed out as you choose, and keeps that the same;
  • can run at the times you choose, several times a day (and check where those runs happen: on a server, or on your computer);
  • exports the results in an analysis-friendly format, so you can line them up with your ad reports;
  • shows trends over time, so changes stand out without building charts yourself.

Some tools read the page at the moment you check; others store a snapshot taken earlier, or show an average over several days. For ads, which change during the day, a fresh check or a set of scheduled checks is the more useful kind.

How do I read the results?

  • No ad found does not mean the campaign is off. The ad may have been out of budget, outside its schedule, outbid at that moment, or not shown for that location or sign-in choice.
  • Compare like with like: same marketplace, same Deliver-to postcode, same location the search is made from, same sign-in choice, similar time of day. A VA searching from another country will see different ads from you, even with the same Deliver-to address.
  • Use the console and the page together. If top-of-search impression share is low and your checks rarely show the ad at the top of the page at the hours that matter, review the bid and the top-of-search placement adjustment. If the share is high but afternoon checks miss the ad, look at budget and ad schedule first.
  • Look at organic and sponsored side by side. An ad sitting just above your own organic listing may be paying for a click you would have got anyway; an ad on a term where you rank on page 3 may be your only visibility there. Either can be the right call; seeing both is what lets you decide.
  • Read competitors the same way, as signals rather than bids. Note which ad types a competitor runs on each key term, how high they appear and when. A top position can come from bid, relevance or sales history. An ad present in the morning and gone later may mean a budget running out; one that appears only later in the day fits an ad schedule or a bid change. Comparing brands you already know are strong or weak (from sales estimates, reviews or your own market knowledge) shows which ad patterns go with success, and where your own approach differs.

Quick questions

Does Amazon show my ad's position on the search page?

Not per search. The console reports placement groups, top-of-search impression share, and impression share and impression rank for search terms, for your own ads.

Is search term impression rank the same as ad position?

No. It ranks advertisers by how many impressions they won on a search term, within that ad type. Position is where the ad sat on a given results page.

Can I track my competitors' sponsored ads?

Yes. Anything a shopper can see, you can record for any ASIN: which ad types a competitor runs on your key terms (product tile, product video, brand banner, brand video, store), how high those ads appear, and at what times of day. That is presence, not their bid, but tracked over weeks for strong and weak competitors alike it shows which advertising patterns go with success on those terms, and how your own compares.

Is this useful for product research or a launch?

Yes. Before or during a launch, checking the main search terms in a niche shows how crowded the first page is with ads, which ad types the leading brands use, and where organic results start. Cost and conversion still come from your own tests and the console, but the page view helps you choose which terms and ad types to test first.

How often should I check?

Start often, then settle. For a week or two, check your key terms several times a day to see how much positions normally move. That spread tells you how many checks to combine into one reading (for example, a daily average of several checks) and how often to check after that. Keep checks at the times of day your sales come in, and use a tool that lets you choose both the timing and the averaging.

Should I check signed in or signed out?

Signed out for a view not tied to one shopping account's history; signed in when you need to see the ads tied to that shopping account, which in our checks included more Sponsored Products video. Choose one and keep it.
