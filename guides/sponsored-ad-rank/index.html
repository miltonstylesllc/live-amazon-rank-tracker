Guides for checking Amazon keyword rank

How do I track where my sponsored ads rank in Amazon search?

Updated November 9, 2026 · Milton Styles LLC

Short answer

Amazon's ad console does not tell you which slot your ad took on a particular search. It reports performance by placement group (top of search, rest of search, product pages), the share of top-of-search impressions you won, and your impression share and impression rank among advertisers for a search term.

To see where your ad actually appears, you have to look at the search results page itself: by hand, or with a tool that reads the page and records each placement. Fix the delivery location and sign-in state, and check more than once a day, because the ads on a page change with both and with the time of day.

What does Amazon's ad console tell you about ad position?

Three reports come close to "where does my ad show", and each answers a different question:

Report or metricWhat it tells youWhat it does not
Placement report (Sponsored Products)Impressions, clicks, spend and sales split by placement group: top of search, rest of search, product pagesWhich slot within the group, or for which keyword
Top-of-search impression shareThe share of top-of-search impressions your campaign wonWhere you showed when you were not at the top, or on which searches
Search term impression share report (Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands)Your share of a search term's ad impressions, and your impression rank: how you place among all advertisers by impressions on that termYour position on the page. An impression rank of 1 means you won the most impressions, not that you held slot 1

Top of search is the sponsored placement at the top of the first page of results; rest of search is every other search placement; product pages are placements on product detail pages and are not a search position at all. Amazon describes the impression share report in its launch note.

These are summaries over many searches. None of them can tell you that your ad was the fourth result on page 1 for "garlic press", delivering to Los Angeles, at 9 a.m. That is the question most sellers are asking when they say "ad rank".

Why is a sponsored position a moving target?

Ads are chosen for each search, so there is no single fixed ad rank for a keyword. What a shopper sees changes with:

  • Delivery location. The Deliver-to address (zip or postcode) changes both the organic results and the ads.
  • Where the search comes from. Searching the same marketplace from another state or country can show different results.
  • Sign-in. Signed-in searches follow the account's shopping history, and some ad types (some Sponsored Product video placements, for example) appear mainly in signed-in searches.
  • Time of day. Budgets run out, ad schedules switch campaigns on and off, and competitors' bids change during the day.
  • Device. The app and the mobile site lay out results differently from a computer.

So a single check is a snapshot. A useful sponsored rank is a series of checks made the same way.

How do I check my sponsored ad position by hand?

  1. Choose a sign-in state. Use a private window (signed out) for a view that is not tied to one account's shopping history, or stay signed in when you need to see ads tied to an account. Keep the same choice every time.
  2. Set Deliver-to to a postcode in the region you care about, and keep it the same between checks.
  3. Search the keyword on the marketplace site you advertise in.
  4. Count the slots from the top, ads included: the brand banner or collection at the top, sponsored product tiles mixed into the grid, video placements, and carousels further down. Note the page number too.
  5. Write down the keyword, time, page, position and placement type, then repeat at other times of day.

Do not click your own ads while checking. Every click costs you money, and clicks and views can feed into what Amazon shows you next time.

Checking by hand works for a handful of keywords. It gets slow beyond that, and your own searching history starts to colour the results when you are signed in.

How should I count positions?

Pick one counting rule and stick to it. A common rule is to count every slot in order, ads included, and record the placement type next to the position. A brand banner or a carousel counts as one slot, however many products it holds. For organic results it also helps to keep a second count that skips the ads, because the number of ads above your listing changes from search to search.

How do I track sponsored rank for many keywords?

Use a tool that loads the search pages and records every placement it finds. Whatever you choose, check that it:

  • records each placement type separately (sponsored product, brand banner or collection, video, carousel, store), not only "sponsored";
  • lets you set the delivery location, so checks are comparable;
  • uses a sign-in state you can see and keep fixed;
  • can run at the times you choose, several times a day (and check where those runs happen: on a server, or on your computer);
  • exports the raw results, so you can line them up with your ad reports.

Some tools read the page at the moment you check; others store a snapshot taken earlier, or show an average over several days. For ads, which change during the day, a fresh check or a set of scheduled checks is the more useful kind.

How do I read the results?

  • No ad found does not mean the campaign is off. The ad may have been out of budget, outside its schedule, outbid at that moment, or not shown for that location or sign-in state.
  • Compare like with like: same marketplace, same postcode, same sign-in state, similar time of day.
  • Use the console and the page together. If your top-of-search impression share is low and your checks rarely find the ad at the top of page 1 at the hours that matter to you, that is a reason to review the bid or placement adjustment for that campaign. If the share is high but your checks miss the ad in the afternoon, look at budget and ad schedule first.
  • Look at organic and sponsored side by side. An ad above your own organic listing on the same search is worth knowing about when you decide where to spend.

Quick questions

Does Amazon show my ad's position on the search page?

Not per search. The console reports placement groups, top-of-search impression share, and impression share and impression rank for search terms.

Is search term impression rank the same as ad position?

No. It ranks advertisers by how many impressions they won on a search term. Position is where the ad sat on a given results page.

How often should I check?

For ads, at least at the times of day your sales come in. Several checks a day show changes from budgets, ad schedules and competitors that one daily check misses.

Should I check signed in or signed out?

Signed out for a view not tied to one account's history; signed in when you need to see ads tied to an account. Choose one and keep it.
