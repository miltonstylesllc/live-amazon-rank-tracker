Questions about checking Amazon keyword rank

Updated October 22, 2026

How do I check my Amazon keyword rank?

Install Live Amazon Rank Tracker from the Chrome Web Store and open it. Paste one or more ASINs and the keywords you care about, choose the marketplace and a search depth (up to 3 Amazon pages per keyword by default, up to 7 if you set it; the check stops early when Amazon has fewer pages), and click Check ranks.

The extension checks today’s Amazon search for each keyword in the background, using your browser’s Amazon session; no Amazon pages open on your screen while it works. It then shows where each ASIN appears, and you can save the results as CSV or Excel.

Is it free?

Yes. The free version checks up to 200 keywords per run in all 13 marketplaces, with organic Rank, Absolute rank, and CSV or Excel export.

Every new user also gets a 14-day full Pro trial, which starts the first time you open the extension. No card is needed and there is nothing to cancel; after 14 days you stay on Free unless you upgrade. Pro ($28 a month or $249 a year) adds sponsored and brand placements, postcode checks, unlimited keywords, Schedule and Trends.

What is Absolute rank, and how is it different from traditional Rank?

Traditionally, Rank is the position in Amazon’s results list, ads included. An ad block such as a collection, carousel or store counts as one position, and the order follows Amazon’s results list, which can differ slightly from the grid on your screen. Absolute rank counts organic results only.

The number of ads above your listing is noisy. It changes with the Deliver-to location, the location the search is made from, whether the session is signed in, the keyword, and the time of day. So Rank can make large moves even when your organic position barely changed. Absolute rank usually stays within a few places when Rank jumps, which makes it the better number for tracking organic progress over time. Every organic row includes both.

Can I track Amazon keyword rank automatically on a schedule?

Yes, with the Pro Schedule tab in the extension. Create jobs with their own marketplace, ASINs and keywords, choose the days and times, and each run saves the same CSV as a manual check to a folder you pick or to Downloads.

Want several readings a day? Create a morning, a midday and an evening job with the same ASINs and keywords. The Pro Trends tab averages same-day runs into one value in its Daily view; its Hourly view keeps each run separate.

Keep your computer on and Google Chrome running; Chrome can be minimized, the extension tab can be closed, and your PC can be locked. Quitting Chrome or putting the computer to sleep pauses scheduled runs.

How do I see keyword rank trends over time?

Save each check (or let Schedule save each run) as a CSV in one folder. The Pro Trends tab in the extension loads that folder and charts every placement type over time, with filters, sorts for the biggest movers, and export of any view. On Free, the Excel companion workbook reads the same folder and plots rank over time.

Why is my rank here different from my other rank tracker?

Most often because the two tools measure different timeframes:

  • Live check (this extension): reads Amazon search at the moment you run the check, so the number is today’s current position.
  • Stored data (most subscription tools): saves a snapshot of the search once a day, or a few times a day, so the number can be hours old. Some of the big all-in-one suites instead show an average of several days. That hides the day-to-day changes you usually want to see, can lag a real change by days or weeks, and can keep showing a rank for weeks after a product has gone out of stock.

Location and sign-in also change what Amazon shows, so two tools checking at the same minute can still differ (see why results differ between people and devices).

Which ad placements does it detect?

Organic results plus sponsored product, collection, carousel, store, and video placements (brand video and sponsored product video are recorded as separate rows). CSV and Excel exports have one row per placement type, so you can see where each of your ads appears next to your organic rank. Free shows organic Rank and Absolute rank; sponsored and brand placements are Pro (and included in the trial).

When the numbers look different

Amazon search changes with location, sign-in, device and time. These answers explain what you are seeing.

Why does my Rank number jump around from day to day?

Usually because the ads above your listing changed, not because your organic position moved much. In one of our tests (United States, two checks 15 minutes apart, about 25,000 organic results found in both), Absolute rank was within 3 places of the first check 96% of the time; Rank only 61% of the time. That is one marketplace and a short gap; over days your organic position moves too, and Absolute rank shows that movement without the ad noise.

Track Absolute rank for organic progress, and use Rank to see what a shopper sees on the page. See what Absolute rank is.

Why do my well-ranked ASINs sometimes appear and disappear from the results?

Usually because the ASIN belongs to a variation family, and for that search Amazon showed a different child of the family. A well-ranked child is found again in most checks, but across a long keyword list a few will seem to vanish on almost every recheck.

In two months of checking one brand’s catalog three times a day on amazon.com (16 child ASINs in 6 families, about 5,500 keywords, signed out), a child in the top 30 was found again 4 hours later about 97% of the time, and the family as a whole (any of its children) more than 99% of the time. When a child did go missing, a sibling from the same family was still ranking in the top 30 for that keyword about three times out of four. “Missing” here means not found anywhere in the first three pages of results.

Track every child ASIN in the family and look at the family’s best position before reading a disappearance as a ranking loss. On Pro, the Schedule can check several times a day, and the Daily view in Trends averages those checks.

What is the difference between a signed-in and a signed-out session?

Even with no Amazon tab open, your browser may still be signed in to Amazon in the background. To check, open the Amazon site for your marketplace: “Hello, sign in” at the top means you are signed out; your name means you are signed in. Sign-in status can differ between Amazon marketplace sites: in our own browser we were signed in on amazon.com and amazon.ca but signed out on amazon.com.mx and the European sites, so check the site for the marketplace you track.

Signed in, Amazon tailors results to your personal shopping history. Signed out, results are less tied to one account and closer to what a typical shopper sees, though Deliver-to still changes them. Sign-in also strongly affects which ad placements Amazon shows and how many (some Sponsored Product video placements, for example, appear mainly when signed in), so it changes the sponsored results you measure.

We usually recommend checking signed out for organic comparisons, and signed in when you need to see ads tied to an account. Test both, then keep the same choice each time.

Why does my rank look different from what my team, my VA, or my phone shows?

Amazon tailors search results to the Deliver-to location, the location the search is made from, and the signed-in account (or none). Delivering to New York or to Los Angeles, searching from Texas or from the Philippines, and searching signed in or signed out can each show very different organic and sponsored results for the same keyword at the same moment. In our own checks of one marketplace from different countries, Rank differed a lot while Absolute rank stayed stable.

A phone adds two more differences: the Amazon app is usually signed in, so results follow your personal shopping history, and phone pages can show a different number of results than a computer, which shifts positions.

For comparable numbers, agree on one setup: the same marketplace, the same sign-in choice, and with Pro the same zip or postcode.

If you do not type a postcode and your browser’s Deliver-to is unset or in another country, the extension sets a standard city for that marketplace for the check. With Pro (and the trial), a typed zip or postcode is set for the check and confirmed on the search page before results are saved; the check stops with a clear message if Amazon rejects the postcode. Afterwards your previous location is restored when it was a local postcode. Amazon Netherlands sets delivery by country only.

Why are some of my sponsored placements not detected, even though I run those ads?

Amazon decides which ads appear on each search, and that changes with the Deliver-to location, the location the search is made from, and sign-in, including which types of placement appear at all. Sponsored Product video placements, for example, often do not appear in signed-out searches. Bids, budgets and ad schedules matter too: an ad that is out of budget or outbid at that moment is not on the page.

To compare, check again signed in or at a different time of day, and make sure you are on Pro or the trial: Free shows organic placements only.

What happens if my ASIN is not on the pages checked?

The keyword is still listed, and its rank result cell is left empty, so you can see that the ASIN was searched for and not found within the pages you chose. Raise the search depth (up to 7 pages) to look further down. For narrow keywords Amazon sometimes offers fewer pages than your setting; the extension checks as many as Amazon shows. On Free only organic results are recorded, so an ASIN that appears only as an ad also shows an empty rank.
